# The Warden's Dashboard

The Dashboard puts every Warden table on a button: encounters by terrain,
weather by season, what an NPC wants, what a monster looks like. It also holds
the clock controls and the generators.

Only the Warden (GM) can open it.

---

## Opening it

Click the **clipboard** in the Token controls, down the left of the map, below
the skull that opens **Warden's Damage**.

The window reopens on the tab you left. Drag its edge to resize it. The
**Pop Out** arrow in its title bar moves it into its own browser window, for a
second monitor; put it back from the **⋮ menu**.

## The clock

Above the tabs is today: the date, the watch, and the season and weather when
there are any. Under that are the buttons that move the clock: **Back a
Watch**, **Advance a Watch**, **Advance a Day**, **To Next Morning** and **Set
the Date…**. Everyone at the table sees the change on the calendar bar across
the top of their screen.

With the Vald calendar on there is also a **Calendar** button, which opens the
month.

**Roll Today's Weather** rolls the weather table for the current season. Its
card always goes to everyone, and the result becomes the day's weather on the
calendar bar. **Set the Weather…** beside it lets you type the weather instead.

**Dungeon Events** and **Wilderness Events** sit under those, so they are in
reach on every tab.

[Keeping Time](keeping-time.md) explains all of it.

## The tabs

| Tab | What is on it |
|---|---|
| **Travel** | Path difficulty, path distance and terrain difficulty; Weather Difficulty and the four seasons' weather, or Vald's four with the Vald calendar on |
| **Encounters** | A table for each of the seven terrains |
| **People** | Reaction, what they want, the six tables that describe an NPC, and Faction |
| **Factions** | Agenda, agent, obstacle, advantages and traits |
| **Monsters** | Appearance, ability, attack and traits |
| **Your Tables** | Every roll table you have made in this world |

**Your Tables** needs no setup. Make a roll table in the sidebar and it appears
there under its own name.

If you copy a shipped table into your world and edit it, its button rolls your
copy.

## Who sees the result

The **Who sees the result** dropdown above the tabs applies to every roll
button on every tab. It opens on **Private to Gamemasters**. Set it to Public
Roll when the party should see what came up. It does not change your usual
dice settings.

Two things ignore it. The **eye** always shows a table to everyone, and **Roll
Today's Weather** always posts publicly.

## Showing a table to the players

Every table button has an **eye** on its right edge. Click the name to roll the
table. Click the eye to show it.

Showing a table opens it on every player's screen and posts a copy to chat, with
every row and its numbers. Nothing is rolled.

## Rolling a whole set

Four tabs carry one button that rolls several tables at once and reports them
as a single result: **Complete Path**, **Complete NPC**, **Complete Faction**
and **Complete Monster**.

These describe; they create nothing. **Complete NPC** gives you a name, a
background, a goal, a quirk, a vice and a virtue, for someone the party will
not meet twice.

## Creating instead

Under **Create** on the People, Factions and Monsters tabs are the generators
the Actors sidebar offers: **Create NPC**, **Create Hireling**, **Create
Faction** and **Create Monster**. Each makes a sheet you can open, edit and
place on a map, or for a faction, a journal entry. Each asks first, in the same
window the sidebar opens, so you can pick a monster's tier, clear **Use random
generation.** for an empty sheet, or name a faction and choose its parts.

## Encounters place their own monsters

An encounter rolled here behaves as one rolled from the compendium does. If its
result names monsters, your copy of the card has an **Add to scene** button that
places them. See [Encounter Tables](encounter-tables.md).

The Encounters tab has no combined button, because a combined result would not
carry Add to scene.

## The party at a glance

The **Party sheet** button at the bottom of the Dashboard, beside Warden's
Damage, opens a table of every character in the Party folder. The **people**
icon in the Token controls opens it too.

Each row is one character: who plays them, their pronouns and background, HP
and the three abilities, armor, gold, slots, Fatigue, whether their shield is
held or slung, the light in their hand, how many rations they have left, how
many hirelings, companions, containers and transports are connected to them,
and their conditions. Click a name to open that character's sheet.

Colour does the warning: amber for HP below max or one slot left; red for HP at
0, an ability below max, no slot left, or no rations; teal for a shield or a
light in hand. A shield or light carried but not in hand shows its symbol
uncoloured. Fatigue and an empty rations count are boxed. Conditions show as D
(Deprived), P (Panicked) and E (Encumbered).

The rows are in marching order. Drag a row onto another to move it there; the
order is saved with the world.

The last column, **Warden's notes**, holds a line of your own on each
character. Only you see it.

Move a character out of the Party folder and it leaves the table; move it back
and it returns.
